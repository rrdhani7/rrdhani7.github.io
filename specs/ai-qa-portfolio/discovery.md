## Different solutions for the selected problem

| Solution | How it solves the problem | Largest assumptions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contextual path | A report offers premium depth, a tool offers access, and a case study offers help | One related offer feels useful instead of intrusive |
| Quiet directory | Content, tools, and consulting have separate pages with simple text links | Visitors will explore without clear prompts |
| Audience path | The home page asks visitors to choose reader, buyer, or tool user | Visitors know which group they belong to when they arrive |

## Proposed first version

Use the contextual path. Keep the home page editorial and simple. Use four main sections: introduction, latest insights, tools lab, and about with proof. Put the premium offer only at the end of a related report. Put product access only on a related tool page. Put consulting only after case studies or inside the about page.

## Interview plan

Interview three people in each main group who made a related decision during the last eight weeks.

- QA or engineering leader who hired help, bought an audit, or changed the QA process.
- Recruiter or hiring manager who shortlisted a senior QA or QA platform candidate.
- QA practitioner who bought a report, template, course, or QA tool.

Ask these questions:

1. Tell me about the last time you looked for QA expertise, a QA product, or a QA candidate.
2. Where did you look first?
3. What made you trust one person or product?
4. What claim made you doubtful?
5. Which result did you need to see?
6. How did you check that result?
7. What did a weak option cost you in time, money, release risk, or reputation?
8. What made you contact, buy, or shortlist the final option?
